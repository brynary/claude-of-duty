# The two remaining blockers

Every judge answered NO to "would this pass as shipped in a two-second
trailer shot", and every one named the weapon, the soldiers, or both. No judge
named the environment; several said it would pass.

## Weapon

- [alley] Re-author the viewmodel surface set: dark anodized albedo (0.04–0.06), a real roughness map splitting metal from polymer, curvature-driven edge wear on rail teeth and mag-well, plus scratch and carbon-fouling decals near the ejection port. This is the largest, nearest, most-scrutinized object in frame and it is currently flat grey plastic.
- [alley] Replace the blob support hand with a rigged gloved mesh — separated fingers wrapping the handguard, knuckle and stitch normal detail, tan nomex albedo — so the lower-left of the viewmodel stops reading as placeholder geometry.
- [alley TRAILER] No — the environment, framing and HUD would hold up for two seconds, but the flat untextured grey receiver and the fingerless blob hand in the lower-right quadrant read as placeholder art instantly to anyone who plays shooters.
- [sunset] Texture the viewmodel. It is currently a flat near-black blockout: author a real PBR set with dark-grey mottled albedo (0.05-0.09), roughness varied 0.3-0.65, a curvature-driven edge-wear mask putting bare steel on the rail tops, charging handle and magazine floorplate, plus a fine normal map for the polymer stipple. Then chamfer the rail teeth and replace the bare sphere and cylinder primitives near the magwell with modelled parts, and split the mitten glove into fingers. This is the single asset an eye lands on first and it is the loudest placeholder tell in the frame.
- [sunset TRAILER] No, but narrowly — the environment, composition and volumetric sunset would pass without question at two seconds, and the HUD reads as genuine CoD-literate design work, but the eye goes straight to the flat untextured black weapon in the lower quarter and clocks it as a placeholder mesh, which drags the whole frame back into demo territory.
- [plaza] Rescale and relight the viewmodel. Shrink the weapon so the receiver spans roughly 30% rather than 45% of frame width and stops burying the lower-right quadrant, align the optic to the aim axis so the scope and the crosshair agree, and feed the weapon the scene's directional sun so it takes a warm rim on its upper edges instead of floating as a flat dark grey cutout over the frame.
- [interior] Replace both viewmodel hands with a properly modelled gloved hand (finger separation, seams, normal and roughness maps), fix the near-white magazine albedo, and extend the camo treatment beyond the suppressor to the rest of the weapon.
- [interior TRAILER] No — the featureless grey lump standing in for the support hand sits directly in the eye's path and the pale untextured cans on the right shelves have no labels or rims, and either one alone would break the illusion well inside two seconds even at trailer speed.
- [weapon] Rebuild the first-person viewmodel: real barrel and flash hider, curved magazine, charging handle and ejection port, bevelled silhouette edges, three separate material IDs with a curvature edge-wear mask, and a properly posed gloved hand whose fingers wrap and deform on the grip. It occupies a quarter of the frame and is currently the lowest-fidelity object in it.
- [weapon TRAILER] No — the gold mannequin standing on the roofline and the unchamfered box rifle filling the right quarter would both register inside the two seconds, even though the plaza behind them would pass on its own.
- [firefight TRAILER] No — the sunlit right-hand facade and the HUD would pass on their own, but the criss-cross straight-line hatch filling the centre of frame and the doubled blocky soldier silhouette both register inside the first half-second and read unmistakably as a WebGL demo.
- [ads] Rebuild the M4 viewmodel to real-world scale and material. Shrink picatinny slot pitch to 5.1mm (currently ~5x oversized), add anodised-aluminium albedo/roughness with edge-wear masks and machining normal detail, fix the smeared UVs on the tan handguard, and add a bloomed red-dot reticle at the optic's optical centre. This one asset dominates the lower third and is the frame's loudest placeholder tell.
- [ads TRAILER] No — the oversized flat-grey rail blocks filling the lower third read instantly as untextured placeholder geometry, and the empty reticle-less optic plus the flat blown-white sky confirm it within the same glance.

## Characters

- [plaza] Replace the enemy mannequin. The box torso, cylinder limbs and cube head at plaza centre is the single object a player clocks fastest, and it is two fidelity tiers below the environment around it. Build a proportioned 1.8m rigged humanoid with a weapon-ready pose and helmet, vest and webbing broken out as distinct silhouette elements with normal-mapped gear. Also replace the ribbon-like red tracer arcs — they read as smooth plastic tubes on an implausible ballistic curve, not as thin hot-cored streaks.
- [plaza TRAILER] No — the enemy soldier is a box-and-cylinder mannequin and the palm crowns are visibly flat alpha cards with stair-stepped edges against the sky, and either one is clocked well inside two seconds even though the surrounding environment art, HUD and grade would otherwise hold up.
- [vista] Replace the rooftop figure at (765,40): it is a blocky low-poly mannequin with cube limbs, a saturated gold torso, and a blown-white square on its chest -- the single most saturated object in the frame and completely out of key with the desaturated environment. Give it a real silhouette (helmet, plate carrier, slung weapon) and an albedo that sits in the scene's palette.
- [vista TRAILER] No -- the mid-right brick facade, wires, poles, and hazed skyline would hold up for two seconds, but the eye lands on the gold blocky mannequin on the roofline and the flat painted-window background slab, and either one alone gives it away as a demo.
- [weapon] Replace the gold artist-mannequin silhouetted on the roofline with a rigged soldier that has a gear silhouette — helmet with cover, plate carrier, pouches, slung weapon, asymmetric limb pose — and give it a contact shadow onto the parapet. Any human figure read against flat sky is the fastest tell in the frame.
- [weapon TRAILER] No — the gold mannequin standing on the roofline and the unchamfered box rifle filling the right quarter would both register inside the two seconds, even though the plaza behind them would pass on its own.
- [firefight] Replace the combatant mesh and fix spawn overlap. The two soldiers at (1255-1340, 505-625) occupy nearly the same position, producing a ghosted double silhouette, and each is a slab-torso mannequin with a box head, rectangular visor, no hands and no held weapon. A skinned humanoid with separated limbs, a weapon in hand, and a textured kit would lift geometry, materials and lighting simultaneously — characters are the subject of this pose and currently the single most amateur asset on screen.
- [firefight TRAILER] No — the sunlit right-hand facade and the HUD would pass on their own, but the criss-cross straight-line hatch filling the centre of frame and the doubled blocky soldier silhouette both register inside the first half-second and read unmistakably as a WebGL demo.
